# Scope: does the CRA apply to Tapir?

The CRA applies to *products with digital elements* that are *made available on the market in
the course of a commercial activity*. Free and open-source software is outside its scope, unless
it is supplied in the course of a commercial activity. Obligations fall on the **manufacturer**:
whoever develops a product and makes it available under their name. For open source, that is
whoever controls releases and the roadmap, not people who only contribute patches.

The European Commission published guidance on these questions in July 2026. It is not legally
binding, but authorities are expected to follow it.

## What speaks for Tapir being in scope

- FoodCoopX develops Tapir, controls its releases and publishes them (GitHub releases, container
  image).
- FoodCoopX offers paid hosting of Tapir to solidarity farms, and the fees also fund development.
- The Commission's guidance lists *monetising services through software* as a commercial
  activity.

Taken together, FoodCoopX would likely count as the manufacturer of Tapir.

## What speaks against it, or is unclear

- Tapir is free software (AGPL-3.0) and available to everyone free of charge. Paid support or
  services alone do not make open source commercial, as long as the software stays freely
  available.
- Pure software as a service, without a product placed on the market, is generally outside the
  CRA. The hosted instances are a service; the question is whether the published releases are a
  product supplied in the course of a commercial activity.
- There is no practice yet for small, cost-covering providers.

## Open-source stewards

The CRA also knows *open-source stewards* (Art. 24): legal persons that support free software
intended for commercial use without being its manufacturer. Their obligations are lighter: a
cybersecurity policy, handling of vulnerabilities and cooperation with authorities, and they
cannot be fined. If FoodCoopX were not a manufacturer, this role might fit instead. The
practical first steps are the same either way.

## Who is not affected

- Solidarity farms that use Tapir are users, not manufacturers.
- Individual contributors who send patches have no CRA obligations.
